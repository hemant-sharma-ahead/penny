Penny — App Icon Directions v5

Two pieces of feedback drove this round. First: real coins read as gold, not green — every previous round kept the coin body in brand green, which is exactly why nothing said "money" clearly. Dark green + gold is also a genuinely premium palette in its own right (British racing green, old-money jewellers' branding), and gold specifically carries strong prosperity associations in an India-first context (gold jewellery, Dhanteras) — so this isn't a compromise on brand, it's brand green as the field the gold coin sits on, same relationship as a medal on a ribbon. Second: a coin is sometimes called a "chip" — not a poker chip, a literal microchip — which opens a real, ownable pun: the same coin can carry a small embossed circuit mark and read as both "Penny" (money) and "Chip" (the AI advisor) at once. All three directions below are a real, dimensional gold coin first — reeded edge, curved-metal shading, specular highlight kept from the version you liked — with the glyph question narrowed down rather than scattered.

An actual struck gold coin with a monogram "P" — the offset dark/light letter pair fakes a real engraved-metal emboss. This is the "no one could possibly misread it" option: gold + coin + initial is how real currency and medallions have worked for centuries. Least inventive of the three, most bulletproof.

The coin's face is struck with a tiny IC-chip die — a square with pin leads on all four sides, three of them lit gold like active connections. Nothing about a square chip die with pin leads resembles a felt-table poker chip; it reads as a literal microprocessor. At a glance it's unambiguously a gold coin (money); look closer and it's also literally a chip (Chip, your AI advisor, built into the money). One mark, both names, no cliché. The pin detail is a 48px+ reward — at 24px it reads as a coin with a small dark square mint-mark, still distinctive, not generic.

No glyph at all — just two overlapping gold coins, a second one peeking out bottom-right, telling "more than one coin, a growing pile" purely through the stack itself. The plainest of the three and, arguably, the most confident: it trusts the gold coin alone to say "money," and the stack to say "growing," without adding a symbol to explain either.

Recommendation: Direction B. It's the only one of the three (or of any round so far) that actually names both halves of the product — Penny and Chip — in a single, unmistakable gold coin, and it's built from simple repeated shapes (a square, straight pin lines, a few dots) so it holds up at every size rather than degrading into an unrecognisable blur. Direction A is the zero-risk fallback if you'd rather not gamble on the chip pun landing; Direction C if you want the icon to say nothing but "money, and more of it over time."
