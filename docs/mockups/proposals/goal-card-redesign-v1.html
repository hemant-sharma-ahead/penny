Leaning toward D (Trajectory hero). It's the one idea here that changes what the card actually tells you, not just how it looks — "On track" vs. a silent 49% is the difference between a status update and a number you have to interpret yourself, and it scales cleanly to every goal shape (short/long horizon, ahead/behind) without needing custom art. B (Roadmap) is the most delightful/story-driven and would be my second pick if you want more personality than D's clean dashboard read. A (Liquid) and C (Blocks) are the most visually bold but risk feeling gimmicky at list-scroll speed once you have 5+ goals on screen — worth prototyping with a full list before picking, not just one card in isolation.

What "on track" would actually mean (Direction D)

Not decorative — computed from the same data already on the card: required monthly pace (targetAmount − effectiveSaved over months remaining) vs. the actual average monthly contribution rate so far (same input Chip's existing "reach this goal in X months" insight already uses). On track = actual pace ≥ required pace; Behind = below it, shown in amber/red instead of green. This is new logic, not currently computed anywhere — happy to scope it as part of implementation once a direction is picked.

Research grounding

  • Jar (India) — literal 3D jar that fills with coins per deposit; physicality reportedly drove +30% completion in their own case study. Direction A is this idea in flat/abstract form.
  • Qapital (US) — illustrated, personalized goal art over a plain bar; considered but not built here since Penny's goals are financial categories, not always mood-board-able ("Emergency Fund" doesn't have an obvious illustration the way "Rainy Day" does).
  • Monarch Money — explicit On track/Ahead/At risk status computed from trajectory, not just %. Direction D's core idea.
  • CRED (NeoPOP) — hard shadows, sharp edges, "engineered object" material language. Direction C borrows this treatment for a stacked-block hero.
  • INDmoney — data-dense but visually plain rings; the "current" card here is already basically this pattern, which is exactly why it reads as generic.
