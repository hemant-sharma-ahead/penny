Two separate decisions below: (1) how the ACTIVE tab reads visually — currently just a color change + thin underline, which you flagged as too subtle; (2) how a user discovers there are MORE tabs off-screen on a narrow/scrollable strip, plus your own auto-center-on-select idea. Both apply to the same shared TabStrip.tsx (Expenses, Goals, Timeline) — Portfolio's hand-rolled tabs would adopt whichever direction you pick too, for consistency.

My recommendation: Option B. Option C (filled pill) is a genuinely different component language — a segmented control, not a tab strip — and would read as a mode-switcher rather than in-page navigation; bigger visual change than "make the active state less subtle" calls for. Option D is close to B but the tinted-background-with-square-corners looks slightly unfinished against this app's already-rounded visual language elsewhere. Option B keeps the exact same tab-strip idiom users already know, just gives the active one real visual weight (bold text, a genuinely thicker underline) instead of a color shift that's easy to miss at a glance — the actual complaint.

My recommendation: the auto-center + edge-fade combination (Part 2, middle option) — your own idea, plus one small addition. Auto-centering alone (your original proposal) is the right core mechanic — the motion itself teaches the user there's more, and edge tabs naturally stay anchored since there's nowhere further to center toward (see the 3-step strip above). The edge fade is a small free addition on top: even BEFORE any tap, a first glance at the strip shows a soft fade at the right edge, hinting "there's more" without needing to touch anything first. The alternative (fade-only, no auto-scroll) is simpler to build but weaker — it never actively surfaces a tab you haven't scrolled to yet, which is the actual complaint (you can miss "Lumpsum & CAGR" exists at all if you never scroll).

One open question for you: should auto-centering happen only on a deliberate TAP (selecting a new tab), or also once on initial mount if the default-selected tab isn't the first one (e.g. Full Ledger's own screens, or anywhere a tab strip could load with a non-first tab pre-selected)? I'd lean toward tap-only for v1 — simpler, and every current `TabStrip` consumer defaults to its first tab anyway — but flagging it as a real edge case worth deciding explicitly.
