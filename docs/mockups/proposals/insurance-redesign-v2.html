What's new here vs v1
  • "Premium payment schedule" block appears right after Payment frequency, only for Term/Life, and disappears for Single premium (a lump sum has no recurring due date) — try switching frequency to "Single" and watch it vanish.
  • Next premium due auto-computes from Start date + the selected frequency's interval; tapping it directly flips to "(custom)" for a user whose real auto-debit date has drifted from the pure calendar math — same auto/custom mechanic as End date.
  • Grace period caption is informational only, sourced from the IRDAI Master Circular (see §③) — never a field the user fills in.
  • Limited pay now actually reveals a years chip-row + a live preview line stating both countdowns side by side (years paying vs. years covered) — v1 only described this in a caption; v2 builds it.
  • Switching type now visibly, silently resets the insurer field (no confirm dialog) — confirmed acceptable; the caption below Insurer always names the new active scope so it's never a silent surprise.
  • "Other" insurer now shows a "Used before" quick-pick chip row plus a live-filtered suggestion dropdown as you type — same visual language as the app's real PersonTypeahead component. Full walkthrough in §④.
③ New — researched

Premium payment mechanics — due dates, grace period, revival window

Verified via WebSearch against multiple current sources (not training-data recall) — GoDigit, Axis Max Life, Kotak Life, and PayBima all independently report the same IRDAI grace-period figures; the revival window comes from IRDAI's own June 2024 Master Circular on Life Insurance Products (Ref: IRDAI/ACTL/MSTCIR/MISC/89/6/2024), corroborated by Ditto Insurance and OneAssure.

Policy startpremium 1 paid
Premium duenext cycle
Grace period ends15d (Monthly) / 30d (else) — cover continues till here
Revival window ends5yr non-linked / 3yr ULIP, from missed due date
Payment modeGrace period
Monthly15 days after due date
Quarterly30 days after due date
Half-yearly30 days after due date
Annual30 days after due date
Single premiumN/A — no recurring due date
Policy categoryRevival window
Non-linked (Term, Endowment, Whole Life)5 years from first unpaid premium
Unit-linked (ULIP)3 years from first unpaid premium

During the grace period, full cover (including the death benefit) continues as if the premium were paid. After it lapses uncured, revival typically requires paying overdue premiums with interest and may require fresh medical underwriting — but a successfully revived policy keeps its original sum assured and entry age, not a fresh/older-age repricing. Sources: GoDigit, Axis Max Life, Kotak Life, PayBima, Oquilia (IRDAI Master Circular summary), Ditto Insurance.

scope decision This due-date/grace/revival design is built for Term and Life/ULIP only. Health, Vehicle, Home, Travel, and Other keep today's single annual renewalDate concept unchanged — Indian Health/Motor/Home/Travel premiums are overwhelmingly paid as one payment at renewal, not spread across a sub-annual payment calendar the way Term/Life commonly are. If monthly-pay health plans become common enough to matter, the exact same pattern built here extends to them later.

The 5 Term due-date states
  • On track — calm green badge, plain days-to-due count. This is the resting state almost all the time.
  • Due soon — same badge shape turns amber inside ~7 days, matching the existing DueDateBadge urgency threshold used everywhere else.
  • Grace period — due date has passed but cover is still fully intact; an inline amber caption states the exact grace window and deadline, sourced from §③'s research.
  • Lapsed — beyond grace, cover has stopped; a red caption states the correct revival deadline (Term is always non-linked → 5-year window) and that the original sum assured survives a successful revival.
  • Paid up — Limited Pay's natural end state: all premiums paid, no more dues, cover continues automatically to the original end date. Distinguishes "done paying" from "policy over."

The two progress lines (payment progress bar + "cover duration" plain text) stay visible in every state — a Limited Pay Term policy that's fully "Paid up" still shows "Cover duration: 15 yrs total · till 2041" so the difference between "I'm done paying" and "I'm no longer covered" is never ambiguous.

⑥ For you to weigh in on

Design decisions made, and open questions

Decided in v1 (unchanged)

  • Nominee(s) moved from a universal field to Term/Life-only. Health uses "members covered" instead; Vehicle/Home/Travel/Other don't have one.
  • "Premium payment term" (Regular vs. Limited pay) is distinct from policy duration — Term/Life only, hidden for Single premium. v2 now actually builds the years-picker + preview instead of just describing it.
  • First-year discount is a one-time year-1 modifier (% or flat ₹), not a permanent premium override.
  • Duration presets are type-specific. Health "members covered" is a multi-select chip picker. Vehicle/Home/Travel share one general-insurer list. Tap-card-to-edit stays the one detail-view interaction, no new popup pattern.

Decided this round (were open questions in v1 §5)

  • decidedVehicle insurance fields stay unlinked from Real Assets' own Vehicle-holding insurance fields. Two independent places, no cross-reference — the simplest option, chosen despite the "two sources of truth can drift" risk flagged in v1. Not revisiting unless real usage shows the drift actually causing problems.
  • decidedSwitching insurance type silently resets an already-picked insurer — no confirmation dialog. The scope caption under the field is the only signal, and that's considered sufficient.
  • decidedThe "Till 60/65/70" age-based duration chips read the user's saved profile DOB locally to compute a real end date — confirmed fine as a local, non-AI, on-device calculation (distinct from the AI-context DOB rule, which only restricts what reaches Chip/the Anthropic API).
  • decidedA custom "Other" insurer is remembered as a local suggestion — "Used before" quick-pick chips plus a live-filtered typeahead, reusing this app's existing merchant/tag "Frequent" chip pattern and PersonTypeahead.tsx's exact interaction, not a new fourth suggestion mechanism. Full states in §④.

New this round — premium due date / grace period

  • Scope: Term/Life only — Health/Vehicle/Home/Travel/Other keep the existing single annual renewalDate, unchanged. See §③'s scope-decision callout for why.
  • "Next premium due" is auto-computed from Start date + the selected frequency's interval, and independently user-overridable (tap-to-edit, flips to "(custom)") for real-world auto-debit drift — same interaction shape as End date's auto/custom toggle, deliberately reused rather than inventing a new one.
  • Grace period is shown as an informational caption, never a field — 15 days (Monthly) / 30 days (Quarterly, Half-yearly, Annual), sourced from the IRDAI Master Circular (§③). It's regulatory context, not user-entered data.
  • Limited Pay shows two separate countdowns (a progress bar for years-of-premiums-paid, a plain "cover duration" line for years-of-cover-remaining) rather than one merged bar — conflating them would make a "10 of 10 years paid" policy look incorrectly like it had also stopped being covered.
  • confirmThis app's single life type bundles ULIP (3-year revival window) with non-linked Endowment/Whole Life (5-year window) under one "Life / ULIP" badge. Term is always non-linked (safe to always say "5 years"), but Life's lapsed-state messaging can't currently pick the right number without knowing which sub-product it is. Options: (a) add a hidden linked/non-linked flag to the Life type-specific fields, (b) show a conservative range ("revival typically 3–5 years — check your policy") for Life only, or (c) treat this as acceptable imprecision for v1. Your call.
  • confirmToday's "next due" is pure calendar math from the original start date. Once a real premium payment is late/missed, the *next* next-due should really be computed from the last actual paid date, not just start-date-plus-N-intervals forever. That implies a future "mark this premium as paid" action Penny doesn't have yet — is that an acceptable v1 gap (due dates stay start-date-derived until that feature exists), or does the due-date feature need that logging capability to ship at the same time?
  • confirmA monthly-pay Term policy has 12 due dates a year, versus one annual renewal for everything else. Should "Due soon"/"Grace period" states also trigger their own banner/reminder (like the existing 30-day renewal Banner), or would firing that up to 12×/year per policy be more noise than help? Not designed in this mockup — flagging as the natural next question once due dates themselves are approved.
  • confirmHealth's co-pay stays a single flat % (unchanged from v1) — some real policies have tiered/room-rent-linked co-pay that doesn't reduce to one number. Still fine to leave that nuance to the free-text Notes field?
