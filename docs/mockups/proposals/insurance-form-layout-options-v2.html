Insurance Add/Edit form — 5 flat, dense options, side by side (v2)

Direct feedback on insurance-form-layout-options-v1.html: "All options are bad. Be intuitive please. We have to keep the taps minimal and do not waste space… Give the options side by side in horizontal style please." v1's "Current (shipped)" recap still applies unchanged — not re-added here per this project's own "don't re-add Current every round" convention. Every option below is grounded fresh in PolicyForm.tsx + fields/*.tsx, applied to the same Term case, with Option ① also shown for Vehicle to prove it generalizes.

✕ Multi-step wizard ✕ Accordion / collapsible sections ✕ One-question-at-a-time

All 5 options below are single, flat, always-fully-visible views — no pagination, no expand/collapse-to-reveal, no step gating. Reaching any field costs zero extra taps, only scroll. The differentiator between options is purely how densely/efficiently space is used, not how much is hidden.

Recommendation

Comparison & recommendation

OptionTaps to reach any fieldVertical space vs. shippedLearning curveImplementation risk
① 2-column gridZero — plain scroll~45% shorterNone — familiar boxed inputsLowest — just wrap existing fields in row pairs
② Hero stripZero~35% shorterLow — new tap-to-edit-inline stripMedium — inline-editable strip is new
③ Inline label rowsZero~40% shorterLowMedium — every field's visual shape changes
④ Segmented-heavyFewest real taps (chip vs. keyboard)~30% shorterLow — reuses existing chip/segment componentsLow-medium — a few TextInputs become steppers
⑤ Settings-row listZeroMost compact overallMedium — form reads more like a settings listMedium-high — needs a real tap-to-edit-in-place mechanic per row
recommendation Lead with Option ① (dense 2-column grid), and fold in Option ③'s technique for the handful of fields that can't naturally pair (Duration chips, Notes). This is the most direct answer to exactly what was asked — intuitive (every field still looks and behaves like a normal input, just laid out two-up), minimal taps (nothing gated behind an extra tap — it's still one continuous scroll), and doesn't waste space (pairing Sum Assured+Premium, dates, and Insurer+Plan name alone removes roughly half the vertical rows the shipped form uses today). It's also the lowest-risk to build: no new component, no new interaction model — just restructuring existing field pairs into flex-row gap-3 rows, the same technique PolicyForm.tsx already uses today for Premium+Frequency.

Option ④ (segmented-heavy) is worth adopting as a smaller, independent follow-on regardless of which layout ships — converting "Pay for how many years?" from a free-text box to a stepper/chip row is a genuine tap-and-space win on its own, orthogonal to the grid-vs-list structural choice.

Option ⑤ (settings-row list) is the single most space-efficient of the 5, but changes what this screen visually reads as — from "a form" to "a settings list" — which risks feeling like a different app surface mid-flow. Worth keeping in mind if a future pass wants to push density further, but not the first move.

Option ② (hero strip) I'd deprioritize specifically because a truly-inline-editable strip segment (tap "₹1.0Cr" and have it become an editable field in place, in a slim strip) is a genuinely fiddly interaction to get right on a small touch target — the risk of it feeling finicky outweighs the visual polish it adds over Option ①.
